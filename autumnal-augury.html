Autumnal Augury

Fri 26 October 2018

Filed under Poems

It doesn’t rain much in a pseudo-desert.
Certainly not this rain,
Which doesn’t so much fall as
Insinuate
A gentle and endless spray accumulating in gutters,
Slowly soaking through my shoes.

I find myself fascinated by the urban mirror world
That pools on the street and slides down windows and falls from trees in fat drops
Asphalt becoming a reflecting pool
And I a seer, almost able to make out the future
Almost able to discern insight
Within the impressions
hiding in
puddles.

Maybe someday I’ll fall right in and learn everyone’s secrets.

I could never escape, of course.
I’d be condemned to dwell in a freezing puddle
Dreaming of things I had never known
Until the next sunny day.

The reassuring presence of the mountains changes, when it rains this much.
Suddenly mysterious, wreathed in the low-hanging clouds
They remind me of the ink paintings, from ancient Japan
Of ancient Japanese mountains, the ink defining half-hidden trees and hills.
When I first saw those paintings, I thought they were so realistic
Now, I see reality, and can only think of a painting.

Maybe I fell into a puddle long ago.

I know when I would have, too.
The whole town flooded, five years ago, now
One spring when it rained and rained and rained
Until I was sure we would be washed away
The whole town, wiped right off the map
Just like that
Floating away on a creek that jumped its banks.

That would have been the time. Maybe while marveling at rushing water
Or listening to the boulders crash in the water
Or ducking my head to escape from the rain, the endless rain
I stepped right into a puddle, and here I’ve lived,
Never noticing,
Catching glimpses of the true world whenever it rains.
